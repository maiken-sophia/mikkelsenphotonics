We design and fabricate optical cavities with gaps of a few nanometers, where light is confined to volumes millions of times smaller than a cubic wavelength. In this regime the usual rules of light–matter interaction stop applying: field gradients couple to transitions that are dipole-forbidden, the vacuum becomes strongly anisotropic, and the cavity's own structure — down to the last nanometer — becomes a design variable.

We map these new rules experimentally, using the statistics of thousands of deterministically fabricated devices rather than one device at a time, and use them to create light–matter interactions that have no counterpart in free space.
